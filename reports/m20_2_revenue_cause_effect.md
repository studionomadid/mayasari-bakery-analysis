# M20.2 — Revenue Cause → Effect Analysis

## Purpose

M20.2 translates the observed monthly revenue patterns into evidence-based business explanations.

The analysis does not assume external causes such as seasonality, promotions, holidays, or marketing campaigns unless those factors are directly supported by available evidence.

Instead, the analysis examines measurable revenue drivers:

- transaction volume
- customer participation
- quantity sold
- average transaction value
- gross profit
- gross margin
- month-over-month revenue movement

The objective is to explain not only **what happened**, but also **which measurable business drivers moved with the result**.

---

## Analytical Principle

Revenue is directly related to transaction activity and average transaction value:

```text
Revenue
   |
   +------------------------+
   |                        |
Transaction Volume     Average Transaction Value
   |                        |
Transactions            Revenue per Transaction
   |
Customers
   |
Quantity Sold
```

This decomposition helps distinguish whether revenue changes are primarily associated with more transactions, more customers, changes in basket size, or a combination of these factors.

---

## Finding 1 — December 2025 Was the Strongest Revenue Month

### Result

December 2025 generated the highest monthly revenue at **Rp 77.9M**, compared with the annual monthly average of approximately **Rp 63.5M**.

December also recorded the strongest month-over-month revenue growth at **26.45%**.

### Evidence

December recorded:

- Revenue: **Rp 77,918,800**
- Transactions: **1,209**
- Customers: **636**
- Quantity sold: **3,696**
- Average transaction value: **Rp 64,449**
- Gross profit: **Rp 33,948,100**
- Gross margin: **43.57%**

Compared with November, December increased by:

- Revenue: **26.45%**
- Transactions: from **1,000** to **1,209**
- Customers: from **585** to **636**
- Quantity sold: from **3,085** to **3,696**
- Average transaction value: from **Rp 61,620** to **Rp 64,449**

### Associated Driver / Pattern

The December revenue increase coincided with increases in both transaction volume and average transaction value.

Transaction volume increased by approximately **20.9%**, while average transaction value increased by approximately **4.6%**.

This means the December revenue surge was not explained by a larger basket alone. The dominant measurable movement was the increase in transaction activity, supported by a smaller increase in transaction value.

### Business Interpretation

December represents a period of unusually strong commercial activity. More transactions were completed, more customers participated, more units were sold, and the average value of each transaction also increased.

The available dataset therefore supports an explanation based on **higher transaction activity combined with stronger transaction value**, rather than an unsupported claim about a specific external campaign or seasonal event.

### Business Consequence

If the operational or commercial conditions associated with this period can be identified and reproduced, the business may be able to make high-revenue periods more predictable.

However, the current dataset does not identify the external reason for the December increase. Campaign timing, holiday demand, promotions, product availability, or other factors remain hypotheses that require additional evidence.

### Recommended Action

Management should investigate the underlying December transaction-level data to determine which products, customers, order sizes, and purchasing patterns contributed to the increase.

The objective should be to identify repeatable mechanisms rather than simply treating December as a one-off high-performing month.

## Finding 2 — February 2025 Was the Lowest Revenue Month

### Result

February 2025 recorded the lowest monthly revenue at **Rp 57.1M**.

Revenue declined **4.07%** compared with January.

### Evidence

February recorded:

- Revenue: **Rp 57,073,225**
- Transactions: **941**
- Customers: **579**
- Quantity sold: **2,898**
- Average transaction value: **Rp 60,652**
- Gross profit: **Rp 24,864,925**
- Gross margin: **43.57%**

Compared with January:

- Transactions decreased from **1,019** to **941**
- Customers decreased from **584** to **579**
- Quantity sold decreased from **3,067** to **2,898**
- Average transaction value increased from approximately **Rp 58,384** to **Rp 60,652**

### Associated Driver / Pattern

The February revenue decline occurred despite an increase in average transaction value.

The more important negative movement was lower transaction activity: transactions and quantity sold both decreased.

This indicates that the February weakness was more strongly associated with **lower transaction volume** than with a deterioration in transaction value.

### Business Interpretation

The business generated slightly more revenue per transaction in February, but completed fewer transactions and sold fewer units overall.

Therefore, increasing basket value alone was insufficient to offset the reduction in transaction activity.

### Business Consequence

Periods with weaker transaction volume can materially reduce revenue even when customers who do purchase maintain or increase their average transaction value.

This makes customer activation and transaction frequency important areas for investigation.

### Recommended Action

Management should examine which customer groups reduced activity in February and whether specific products, customer segments, or transaction patterns contributed to the lower volume.

## Finding 3 — October 2025 Showed the Sharpest Monthly Revenue Decline

### Result

October 2025 recorded the weakest month-over-month revenue movement at **-7.86%**.

Revenue decreased from **Rp 63.37M** in September to **Rp 58.38M** in October.

### Evidence

October recorded:

- Revenue: **Rp 58,382,700**
- Transactions: **1,070**
- Customers: **609**
- Quantity sold: **3,063**
- Average transaction value: **Rp 54,563**
- Gross profit: **Rp 25,845,400**
- Gross margin: **44.27%**

Compared with September:

- Transactions decreased from **1,112** to **1,070**
- Customers decreased from **633** to **609**
- Quantity sold decreased from **3,196** to **3,063**
- Average transaction value decreased from approximately **Rp 56,983** to **Rp 54,563**

### Associated Driver / Pattern

October is different from February because both transaction activity and average transaction value moved downward.

The decline was therefore broader than a simple reduction in transaction count.

### Business Interpretation

The October decline suggests weaker commercial activity across two measurable dimensions: fewer transactions and lower revenue generated per transaction.

This makes October a particularly important period for transaction-level investigation.

### Business Consequence

When both transaction volume and transaction value decline simultaneously, revenue can fall more sharply and recovery may require addressing multiple behavioral or commercial factors.

### Recommended Action

Management should compare October with September at customer and product level to identify which customers reduced activity, which products lost volume, and whether transaction-size distribution changed.

## Finding 4 — Revenue Momentum Was Primarily Volume-Driven During Strong Growth

### Result

Several positive-growth months show that revenue improvements were accompanied by increases in transaction activity.

The strongest example is December, where revenue increased **26.45%** month over month.

### Evidence

December increased simultaneously across several activity measures:

- Transactions: **1,000 → 1,209**
- Customers: **585 → 636**
- Quantity: **3,085 → 3,696**
- Average transaction value: **Rp 61,620 → Rp 64,449**

### Associated Driver / Pattern

The evidence indicates that stronger revenue months can result from a combination of more purchasing activity and higher transaction value.

The December pattern particularly emphasizes transaction volume because the increase in transactions was substantially larger than the increase in average transaction value.

### Business Interpretation

Revenue growth should not be evaluated only through total sales. Management should monitor the underlying components of revenue to determine whether growth is coming from customer activity, purchase frequency, basket size, or a combination.

### Business Consequence

A growth strategy that relies only on increasing transaction value may have limited impact if transaction frequency or customer participation remains weak.

### Recommended Action

Management should track revenue together with transactions, active customers, quantity, and average transaction value as a standard revenue-performance dashboard.

---

## Finding 5 — Gross Margin Remained Relatively Stable Across Revenue Movements

### Result

Monthly gross margin remained within a relatively narrow range despite substantial revenue changes.

The monthly range was approximately **43.57% to 44.27%**.

### Evidence

Examples include:

- February: **43.57%**
- June: **44.00%**
- October: **44.27%**
- December: **43.57%**

### Associated Driver / Pattern

Revenue volatility was therefore not accompanied by a comparable structural shift in gross margin.

This suggests that the major observed revenue fluctuations were more closely associated with sales activity than with large monthly changes in gross-margin rate.

### Business Interpretation

The business appears to maintain a relatively stable gross-margin structure while sales volume changes.

This is important because it indicates that improving revenue through additional transaction activity could potentially translate into additional gross profit, provided the underlying margin structure remains stable.

### Business Consequence

Revenue growth that preserves the existing margin structure is more valuable than revenue growth achieved through excessive discounting or unfavorable product mix.

### Recommended Action

Management should continue monitoring both revenue and gross margin together so that growth initiatives do not unintentionally weaken profitability.

## What the Data Does Not Prove

The available analytical dataset does **not** establish that revenue changes were caused by:

- seasonal demand
- holidays
- marketing campaigns
- discounts
- promotions
- stock availability
- external economic conditions

Those explanations may be plausible, but they require additional evidence such as campaign records, inventory data, calendar effects, customer-level event data, or transaction-level promotion information.

The current analysis therefore uses **associated drivers and measurable patterns**, rather than unsupported causal claims.

---

## Management Takeaway

The most important revenue lesson is that Mayasari Bakery revenue movement is strongly connected to measurable transaction activity.

The strongest month, December 2025, combined higher transaction volume, more active customers, more units sold, and a higher average transaction value. The weakest periods show the opposite pattern, particularly October where both transaction activity and average transaction value declined.

This suggests that management should treat revenue growth as a combination of **customer activation, transaction frequency, quantity sold, and basket value**, while protecting the relatively stable gross-margin structure.

The next analytical step should be to investigate these drivers at customer and product level so that management can determine which specific behaviors and products explain the observed monthly movements.
