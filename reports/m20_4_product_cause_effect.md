# M20.4 — Product Economics & Cause–Effect Analysis

## Purpose

This analysis evaluates product-level economic performance across the Mayasari Bakery product portfolio.

The objective is to identify:

- which products contribute the greatest economic value;
- how revenue and gross profit are concentrated across products;
- whether transaction volume corresponds to economic contribution;
- how price, volume, and margin interact at the product level;
- how product categories differ in scale and profitability;
- and which product patterns should inform management prioritization.

The analysis is based on the product performance dataset containing **28 products across 8 categories**.

The analysis focuses on observed associations and economic concentration patterns. It does not attempt to establish causal effects from price, volume, category, or margin.

---

## Analytical Principle

Product performance should not be evaluated using a single metric.

A product can generate high revenue because of:

- high transaction value;
- high transaction frequency;
- high quantity sold;
- or a combination of these factors.

Similarly, a product can generate strong gross profit through:

- high revenue;
- high gross margin;
- or both.

Therefore, product evaluation should consider at least four dimensions:

1. **Scale** — revenue and transaction contribution.
2. **Economic value** — gross profit contribution.
3. **Efficiency** — gross margin.
4. **Volume** — quantity and transaction frequency.

The analysis therefore distinguishes between products that are:

- high-value;
- high-volume;
- high-margin;
- or strategically important because of their portfolio contribution.

---

## Finding 1 — Product Value Was Concentrated in a Small Group of Products

The product portfolio contains **28 products** generating approximately **Rp 761.8M in historical revenue** and **Rp 334.3M in gross profit**.

Economic contribution was concentrated among the highest-performing products.

The three largest products by revenue were:

| Product | Category | Revenue | Revenue Share | Gross Profit | Profit Share |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Birthday Cake | Cake | Rp104.15M | 13.67% | Rp43.39M | 12.98% |
| Brownies Box | Cake | Rp66.23M | 8.69% | Rp30.25M | 9.05% |
| Paket Hampers Mini | Hampers | Rp58.00M | 7.61% | Rp22.60M | 6.76% |

Together, these three products contributed approximately **29.98% of total revenue** and **28.79% of total gross profit**.

The five largest products contributed approximately **42.60% of revenue** and **40.89% of gross profit**.

This indicates that the portfolio is diversified across 28 products, but economic contribution remains materially concentrated in a smaller group of leading products.

### Management implication

The leading products should receive disproportionate attention in:

- stock availability;
- production planning;
- quality control;
- pricing review;
- promotion planning;
- and service reliability.

A disruption affecting a high-contribution product can have a larger economic impact than a disruption affecting a low-contribution SKU.

## Finding 2 — High-Value Products Generated More Economic Value per Transaction

Transaction frequency alone did not explain product economic contribution.

**Birthday Cake** generated approximately:

- **Rp104.15M revenue**;
- **Rp43.39M gross profit**;
- **369 transactions**;
- **620 units sold**;
- **41.66% gross margin**.

Its observed average economic contribution was approximately:

- **Rp282K revenue per transaction**;
- **Rp117.6K gross profit per transaction**.

By comparison, **Roti Cokelat** was one of the highest-volume products:

- **1,420 transactions**;
- **2,261 units sold**;
- **Rp20.72M revenue**;
- **Rp8.96M gross profit**;
- **43.26% gross margin**.

The contrast demonstrates that a product can generate substantially greater economic value without having the highest transaction frequency.

At the product level, transaction frequency and revenue showed a negative correlation of approximately **-0.549**, while quantity and revenue showed a negative correlation of approximately **-0.513**.

These relationships should not be interpreted as causal effects. They indicate that, within this product portfolio, higher volume was not synonymous with higher economic contribution.

### Management implication

Product prioritization should not rely on transaction count or quantity alone.

Management should monitor:

- revenue per transaction;
- gross profit per transaction;
- gross margin;
- total revenue contribution;
- and total gross profit contribution.

This helps distinguish products that create **high economic value per customer transaction** from products that primarily create volume.

## Finding 3 — High-Volume Products Operated at Relatively Lower Margin Levels

Several products combined high sales volume with gross margins below the product-level median of approximately **44.97%**.

The high-volume / lower-margin group included:

| Product | Quantity | Transactions | Revenue | Gross Margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Roti Cokelat | 2,261 | 1,420 | Rp20.72M | 43.26% |
| Bolen Pisang | 2,105 | 1,333 | Rp24.30M | 43.70% |
| Roti Manis | 2,068 | 1,300 | Rp16.96M | 41.46% |
| Roti Keju | 1,879 | 1,174 | Rp18.07M | 41.78% |

These products are operationally important because they generate substantial transaction activity, but their margins are below several lower-volume products.

For comparison, **Kue Sus** generated:

- 1,657 units;
- 1,040 transactions;
- Rp14.39M revenue;
- Rp7.26M gross profit;
- **50.47% gross margin**.

### Management implication

High-volume products should not automatically be treated as the most economically attractive products.

For high-volume / lower-margin products, management should investigate:

- ingredient cost;
- portion size;
- production efficiency;
- packaging cost;
- waste;
- pricing structure;
- and opportunities for bundling or cross-selling.

The objective should be to improve economic efficiency without unnecessarily reducing the volume contribution of products that already have strong customer demand.

## Finding 4 — Higher-Priced Products Carried a Disproportionate Share of Economic Value

The product portfolio was divided into four price bands.

The highest-priced quartile contained **7 products** but contributed approximately:

- **50.67% of total revenue**;
- **49.24% of total gross profit**;
- with **3,672 transactions**.

The lowest-priced quartile contained **8 products** and contributed approximately:

- **14.14% of total revenue**;
- **15.24% of total gross profit**;
- with **8,516 transactions**.

At the product level, price was strongly positively associated with revenue at approximately **0.950** and with gross profit at approximately **0.944**.

At the same time, price was negatively associated with quantity at approximately **-0.637** and transaction count at approximately **-0.653**.

This pattern indicates that higher-priced products generated substantially greater economic contribution despite lower transaction and quantity volume.

### Management implication

Management should not optimize the product portfolio solely for transaction volume.

Higher-value products may deserve stronger attention in:

- premium positioning;
- availability assurance;
- product presentation;
- upselling;
- occasion-based marketing;
- and customer retention initiatives.

However, the observed relationship does **not** establish that increasing prices will automatically increase revenue or profit.

Any pricing change should be validated through:

- historical price variation;
- controlled pricing experiments;
- customer response analysis;
- or A/B testing where feasible.

## Finding 5 — Product Categories Differed Between Scale and Margin Efficiency

Product categories displayed different economic profiles.

| Category | Revenue | Revenue Share | Gross Profit | Profit Share | Gross Margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Cake | Rp259.34M | 34.04% | Rp113.80M | 34.04% | 43.88% |
| Cookies | Rp112.40M | 14.75% | Rp51.69M | 15.46% | 45.99% |
| Pastry | Rp106.06M | 13.92% | Rp46.95M | 14.05% | 44.27% |
| Roti | Rp84.61M | 11.11% | Rp35.52M | 10.62% | 41.98% |
| Hampers | Rp58.00M | 7.61% | Rp22.60M | 6.76% | 38.97% |
| Kue Tradisional | Rp51.40M | 6.75% | Rp25.29M | 7.56% | 49.19% |
| Snack Box | Rp50.65M | 6.65% | Rp19.80M | 5.92% | 39.09% |
| Donat | Rp39.33M | 5.16% | Rp18.62M | 5.57% | 47.34% |

**Cake** was the largest category by both revenue and gross profit, contributing approximately **34.04%** of each.

However, **Kue Tradisional** achieved the highest category gross margin at approximately **49.19%**, followed by Donat at **47.34%** and Cookies at **45.99%**.

This demonstrates that category scale and margin efficiency are not identical dimensions.

### Management implication

Management should maintain separate strategic roles for categories:

- **Cake** — scale and economic anchor.
- **Cookies** — strong margin and meaningful scale.
- **Kue Tradisional** — margin efficiency opportunity.
- **Donat** — strong margin with smaller revenue contribution.
- **Hampers / Snack Box** — meaningful revenue contribution but comparatively lower margins.
- **Roti** — high transaction volume with comparatively lower margin efficiency.

Category decisions should therefore balance **scale, margin, and strategic role** rather than ranking categories using revenue alone.

---

## Finding 6 — Lower-Revenue Products Were Not Necessarily Economically Weak

The lowest-revenue products included:

- Klepon — Rp10.72M revenue and 49.44% gross margin;
- Donat Keju — Rp11.52M revenue and 45.91% gross margin;
- Lemper Ayam — Rp12.84M revenue and 48.79% gross margin;
- Risoles — Rp13.46M revenue and 48.01% gross margin;
- Kue Sus — Rp14.39M revenue and 50.47% gross margin.

Several of these products generated a smaller share of revenue while maintaining relatively strong gross margins.

Therefore, low revenue should not automatically trigger product discontinuation.

A product may have:

- strategic customer relevance;
- strong margin;
- seasonal demand;
- cross-selling value;
- or operational importance

that is not fully captured by revenue ranking alone.

### Management implication

Before discontinuing low-revenue products, management should evaluate:

1. gross margin;
2. gross profit contribution;
3. transaction frequency;
4. customer segment dependence;
5. seasonality;
6. cross-selling potential;
7. production complexity;
8. and inventory/waste characteristics.
