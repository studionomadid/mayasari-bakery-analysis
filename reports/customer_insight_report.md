# Mayasari Bakery Customer Insight Report

> Evidence-backed customer economics, behavior, retention, and opportunity assessment

---

## Executive Summary

The customer analysis indicates that Mayasari Bakery's customer economics are meaningfully concentrated in higher-value customers, while a material share of customer value is associated with Rescue and At-Risk populations. Platinum CLV share: 44.52%; top 25 CLV share: 11.67%. Rescue CLV share: 24.51%; Rescue Platinum customers: 84. At-Risk CLV share: 18.78%; Platinum: 55; Gold: 40. Gross margin: 43.88%; top 10 gross-profit share: 5.51%. The resulting management priority is to protect economically valuable relationships while systematically developing customers with further economic potential.

## Key Findings

### 1. Customer Value Concentration

**Insight ID:** `customer_value_concentration`  
**Category:** Customer Economics  
**Priority:** **High**

**Evidence**

> Platinum CLV share: 44.52%; top 25 CLV share: 11.67%.

**Interpretation**

Customer economic value is materially concentrated within the Platinum tier.

**Business implication**

Platinum customers contribute 44.52% of annualized CLV, while the top 25 customers contribute 11.67%. Retention performance among the highest-value customers therefore has disproportionate economic importance.

**Recommended action**

Protect high-value customer relationships while developing lower-tier customers to reduce dependency.

### 2. CLV Tier Economics

**Insight ID:** `clv_tier_economics`  
**Category:** Customer Economics  
**Priority:** **High**

**Evidence**

> Platinum CLV share: 44.52%; Gold CLV share: 23.92%.

**Interpretation**

Platinum customers contribute 44.52% of annualized CLV, compared with 23.92% from Gold customers.

**Business implication**

The upper CLV tiers represent the economically strongest customer population and should receive differentiated retention and development treatment.

**Recommended action**

Use differentiated retention and development strategies according to customer economic value.

### 3. RFM Customer Quality

**Insight ID:** `rfm_customer_quality`  
**Category:** Customer Behavior  
**Priority:** **Medium**

**Evidence**

> Largest segment: Potential Loyalists; customer share: 27.88%; average RFM: 11.96.

**Interpretation**

The largest observed RFM segment is 'Potential Loyalists', representing 27.88% of customers with an average RFM score of 11.96.

**Business implication**

Customer behavior is heterogeneous, so engagement strategies should be differentiated by observed RFM characteristics rather than treating the entire customer base uniformly.

**Recommended action**

Match engagement strategy to observed customer behavior rather than applying one strategy to all customers.

### 4. Customer Opportunity Priority

**Insight ID:** `customer_opportunity_priority`  
**Category:** Customer Opportunity  
**Priority:** **High**

**Evidence**

> Priority 1-3 customers: 362; priority-group CLV share: 49.30%.

**Interpretation**

Priority 1-3 opportunity groups contain 362 customers and account for 49.30% of annualized CLV.

**Business implication**

The economically relevant intervention population should be prioritized according to both customer value and the existing behavioral opportunity classification.

**Recommended action**

Prioritize economically meaningful intervention groups, especially high-value customers showing declining engagement.

### 5. Rescue Customer Economics

**Insight ID:** `rescue_economics`  
**Category:** Customer Opportunity  
**Priority:** **High**

**Evidence**

> Rescue CLV share: 24.51%; Rescue Platinum customers: 84.

**Interpretation**

Rescue customers account for 24.51% of annualized CLV, indicating that the Rescue group contains economically meaningful customers.

**Business implication**

The Rescue population includes 84 Platinum customers, so customer re-engagement should not be treated as a low-value blanket campaign.

**Recommended action**

Prioritize targeted re-engagement where expected customer economic value justifies intervention.

### 6. High-Value At-Risk Customers

**Insight ID:** `high_value_at_risk`  
**Category:** Retention  
**Priority:** **High**

**Evidence**

> At-Risk CLV share: 18.78%; Platinum: 55; Gold: 40.

**Interpretation**

At-Risk customers account for 18.78% of annualized CLV, indicating meaningful economic exposure within the observed customer base.

**Business implication**

The At-Risk population contains 55 Platinum and 40 Gold customers. Retention attention should therefore be concentrated on economically valuable members of this group.

**Recommended action**

Protect high-value at-risk relationships with targeted retention actions before broad acquisition spending.

### 7. Customer Profitability

**Insight ID:** `customer_profitability`  
**Category:** Customer Economics  
**Priority:** **Low**

**Evidence**

> Gross margin: 43.88%; top 10 gross-profit share: 5.51%.

**Interpretation**

The customer base generates a 43.88% overall gross margin, while the top 10 customers account for 5.51% of gross profit.

**Business implication**

Customer value should be assessed using gross-profit contribution rather than revenue alone, because revenue concentration does not necessarily represent profitability concentration.

**Recommended action**

Prioritize customers and segments based on profitable economic contribution rather than revenue alone.

## Strategic Priorities

- 1. Protect high-value customer economics — High priority. Platinum customers contribute 44.52% of annualized CLV, while the top 25 customers contribute 11.67%. Retention performance among the highest-value customers therefore has disproportionate economic importance.

- 2. Address economically meaningful Rescue customers — High priority. The Rescue population includes 84 Platinum customers, so customer re-engagement should not be treated as a low-value blanket campaign.

- 3. Prioritize high-value retention exposure — High priority. The At-Risk population contains 55 Platinum and 40 Gold customers. Retention attention should therefore be concentrated on economically valuable members of this group.

- 4. Focus customer development on economically relevant opportunity groups — High priority. The economically relevant intervention population should be prioritized according to both customer value and the existing behavioral opportunity classification.

## Retention Recommendations

- Protect Platinum and Gold customers with differentiated retention treatment rather than a uniform customer policy.

- Prioritize economically valuable Rescue customers for targeted re-engagement, with particular attention to Platinum Rescue customers.

- Create a high-value At-Risk retention queue so Platinum and Gold customers receive earlier management attention.

- Use the observed CLV concentration as a management signal for retention planning rather than relying on customer count alone.

- Retention planning should account for the finding that at-risk clv share: 18.78%; platinum: 55; gold: 40.

- The retention program should also recognize that platinum clv share: 44.52%; top 25 clv share: 11.67%.

- Rescue intervention should remain economically selective: rescue clv share: 24.51%; rescue platinum customers: 84.

## Customer Development Recommendations

- Develop lower CLV tiers using differentiated engagement strategies designed around observed customer behavior.

- Use RFM characteristics to identify customers with development potential rather than applying identical campaigns across the customer base.

- Prioritize economically meaningful opportunity groups before allocating broad customer-development resources.

- Evaluate customer-development initiatives using gross profit contribution as well as revenue.

- Upper-tier economics should remain a benchmark for customer development because platinum clv share: 44.52%; gold clv share: 23.92%.

- Customer development should recognize behavioral heterogeneity: largest segment: potential loyalists; customer share: 27.88%; average rfm: 11.96.

- Opportunity prioritization should remain value-aware: priority 1-3 customers: 362; priority-group clv share: 49.30%.

- Profitability should remain part of customer-development evaluation: gross margin: 43.88%; top 10 gross-profit share: 5.51%.

## Opportunity Actions

- Rescue — launch targeted re-engagement for economically valuable inactive or declining customers.

- Protect — prioritize retention actions for customers already demonstrating meaningful economic value.

- Develop — identify customers with observable potential to progress into stronger CLV tiers.

- Grow — strengthen engagement and purchase development for customers showing positive commercial potential.

- Win-back — test focused reactivation strategies using historical value and observed customer behavior.

- Monitor — maintain lightweight monitoring and avoid over-investing before stronger economic signals emerge.

- Review — investigate customer-level economics and behavior before assigning intensive intervention.
